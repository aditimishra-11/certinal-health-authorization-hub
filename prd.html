01 · Problem & opportunity

Healthcare has a permission execution problem, not a consent problem.

DPDP turns patient consent from an informal, paper-heavy ritual into an operational obligation: a hospital must show what notice was given, what consent was captured, for what purpose, whether it was withdrawn, and what it did about it. Today that lives in PDFs, shared inboxes and Excel — captured but never executed or proven. ABDM owns identity and record-sharing consent; EMR/HIS own the record; privacy suites own policy. Nobody owns the layer that acts on a permission and proves it across systems. That layer is the product.

I assume "should we build it?" is yes, under one condition: by going deep on execution for a focused wedge (hospital chains first), not broad on a generic DPDP suite. The full narrative, market and positioning live in the case study; this document is the buildable spec.

02 · How I arrived at the modules

Four modules, each derived from a specific obligation.

I didn't start from a feature wish-list. I started from the four things DPDP forces a hospital to do with a permission — capture it validly, act within its scope, stop when it's withdrawn, and prove all of the above — and built one module per verb. That's the core four-step flow: Capture → Authorize → Withdraw → Prove.

DPDP obligationWhat it forces the hospital to doModule
Notice & valid consent
(S5, S6)		Present an understandable notice and capture free, specific, purpose-bound consent — and keep every version.1 · Consent Lifecycle
Purpose limitation & lawful processing
(S7, S8)		Ensure each use is backed by a valid, scoped permission and route the right approvals before data moves.2 · Authorization Workflow Engine
Right to withdraw
(S6(4)–(6))		Let a patient withdraw as easily as they consented, and cease processing across every system that holds the data.3 · Withdrawal Management
Accountability / being able to prove compliance
(S5, S8)		Prove, on demand, exactly what was permitted, by whom, when — court-ready.4 · Audit Evidence Vault

Everything outside this spine — data discovery, DPIA tooling, vendor risk, cookie/web consent, a DSAR rights console — is real DPDP work, but it's governance breadth, not permission execution. It's where OneTrust and Securiti already win, and it's not where Certinal's strengths sit. So it's deliberately deferred (see v1 scope).

What v1 is for — and what it refuses to be.

Business goals

  • Open a defensible wedge competitors can't easily follow.

Module 1 — Consent Lifecycle6 P08 features

WHY THIS MODULE  DPDP Section 5 (notice) and Section 6 (consent must be free, specific, informed, unambiguous and given by a clear affirmative action, for a specified purpose) require the hospital to present an understandable notice and capture valid, purpose-bound consent in the patient's language — and to keep every version provable.

P0Purpose-Based Consent CaptureFront-desk staff

Capture consent split into discrete, independently-toggled purposes (treatment, diagnostics sharing, research) rather than one blanket form.

As front-desk staff, I want to capture consent broken out by purpose so that each downstream use has its own provable basis under DPDP.
  • Each purpose is an independent opt-in; bundled or blanket consent is not possible.
  • Required vs optional purposes are visually distinct and no purpose defaults to ON.
  • Each captured consent records purpose, data categories, timestamp and the notice version shown.
  • A patient can grant some purposes and decline others and still complete the flow.
P0Vernacular Notice & ConsentPatient (data principal)

Present the notice and consent in the patient's chosen language (English + Eighth-Schedule languages), language chosen before anything else.

As a patient, I want the notice and consent in my own language so that I actually understand what I am agreeing to.
  • Language is selected before any notice text is shown.
  • v1 ships English, Hindi and at least two regional languages.
  • The exact language version shown is stored with the consent record.
  • Switching language never changes the scope or purposes being captured.
P0Assisted & Kiosk Capture for OPDFront-desk staff

Low-literacy-friendly capture on a shared tablet/kiosk, self-serve or staff-assisted, designed for an OPD waiting room.

As front-desk staff, I want to capture consent on a shared device for a patient who can barely use a phone so that nobody is excluded or pushed back to paper.
  • A consent completed on a staff-operated device is still attributed to the patient after patient verification.
  • Interface offers large touch targets, icon-led purposes and a read-aloud option.
  • A standard OPD consent completes in roughly 90 seconds.
  • Staff-assisted sessions record that assistance was given and by whom.
P0Identity-Bound Signature (ABHA / OTP)Patient (data principal)

Bind each consent to a verified identity via ABHA or mobile OTP and sign it with Certinal eSign.

As a DPO, I want every consent bound to a verified patient identity so that it holds up as that specific person's affirmative action.
  • Consent is signed via Certinal eSign and bound to a verified identifier (ABHA where available, else OTP-verified mobile).
  • The signed artifact is immutable once captured.
  • The identity-verification method used is recorded on the consent.
  • An unverified consent cannot reach 'active' status.
P0Version Tracking & Re-ConsentCompliance team / DPO

Every notice or purpose change creates a new immutable version; materially affected patients are flagged for re-consent.

As a DPO, I want every consent version preserved and changes to trigger re-consent so that we can always prove which version a patient agreed to.
  • Editing a notice or purpose set creates a new version; prior versions are never overwritten.
  • Each consent records the exact version the patient agreed to.
  • A material change flags impacted patients for re-consent.
  • Superseded consents are marked as such, not deleted.
P0Queryable Consent RepositoryCompliance team / DPO

A central, searchable store of all consents with status, purpose, language, version and validity.

As compliance, I want to search every consent by patient, purpose or status so that I can answer 'what did this patient agree to' instantly.
  • Search by patient/ABHA, purpose, status and date range.
  • Each record shows purpose, language, version, validity window and current status.
  • A single patient's full consent set exports in one action.
  • Results return in seconds, not minutes.

Module 2 — Authorization Workflow Engine4 P08 features

WHY THIS MODULE  DPDP Section 7 purpose limitation and Section 8(1) accountability require a Data Fiduciary to ensure all processing is backed by valid, purpose-scoped permission and to prove it on demand, so the hospital needs an enforceable execution layer — route, scope, enforce at point-of-use, and evidence — not just stored consent.

P0Purpose-Scoped Grant BuilderCompliance team / DPO

Defines every authorization as a structured grant bound to a specific purpose, data categories, and time/expiry window so processing can never exceed what the patient permitted.

As a DPO, I want every authorization to be tied to a named purpose, specific data categories, and an expiry so that processing is provably limited to what the patient agreed to under DPDP purpose limitation.
  • A grant cannot be created without at least one named purpose, one or more data categories, and a validity window (fixed end-date or event-based, e.g. 'until discharge').
  • Selecting a purpose auto-constrains the selectable data categories to those declared necessary for that purpose; out-of-scope categories cannot be added.
  • Each grant shows a human-readable scope summary ('Cardiology can view labs + imaging for treatment until 31-Dec-2026') visible to staff and patient.
  • A grant past its expiry or event trigger automatically moves to an inactive state with no further processing allowed.
P0Configurable Multi-Party Approval RoutingHospital admin

Routes an authorization request through an ordered or parallel set of approvers (clinician, DPO/ethics, patient) with rules that vary by use-case and data sensitivity.

As a hospital admin, I want to configure who must approve which type of request so that sensitive data sharing always gets the right sign-offs before any data moves.
  • Admins can build a routing rule specifying approvers, order (sequential or parallel), and conditions (purpose, data category, requester role) without code.
  • A request cannot reach 'granted' state until every required approver in the active route has acted.
  • Patient approval can be inserted as a mandatory step in any route, and the patient's decision is binding (a clinician cannot override a patient denial).
  • Each approver sees the exact scope they are approving, and a rejection at any step halts the request with a recorded reason.
P0Use-Case Workflow TemplatesCompliance team / DPO

Prebuilt, editable templates for the common Indian healthcare authorization scenarios — treatment, insurance/TPA claim, caregiver access, external data share, and research — so each use-case ships with correct routing, scope, and notices.

As a DPO, I want ready-made templates per use-case so that front-line teams launch compliant authorizations without reinventing scope and routing each time.
  • V1 ships with at least four templates: insurance/TPA, caregiver/family access, external data share, and research/ethics.
  • Each template pre-fills purpose, default data categories, validity, and the required approver route, and remains editable before launch.
  • Selecting a template enforces its mandatory steps (e.g. research template forces a DPO/ethics approval step that cannot be removed).
  • Templates are versioned so the exact template version used for any grant is recoverable later.
P0Point-of-Use Enforcement CheckClinician

A real-time permission check that EMR/HIS and downstream systems call before any data access or share, returning allow/block based on the live grant — turning permission into enforcement, not paperwork.

As a clinician, I want the system to tell me in real time whether I'm permitted to access or share a patient's data so that I never act outside what the patient authorized.
  • A data-use attempt is evaluated against the active grant's purpose, data category, requester, and validity, returning a clear allow or block decision.
  • If no valid grant exists or the requested use falls outside scope, the action is blocked and the reason is shown to the user.
  • An expired, withdrawn, or out-of-window grant produces a block even if the data was previously accessible.
  • Every check (allow and block) is recorded with timestamp, requester, purpose, and outcome.

P1Guardian and Delegated Authorization— Handles authorizations granted by a verified guardian for a minor or by a delegate/caregiver for an incapacitated adult, binding the grant to the delegate's verified identity and relationship.Front-desk staff

Module 3 — Withdrawal Management5 P08 features

WHY THIS MODULE  DPDP Section 6(4)-(6) requires that a Data Principal be able to withdraw consent "as easily as it was given," that the Data Fiduciary cease processing within a reasonable time, and that this cascade to its Data Processors — so a hospital needs an execution-and-evidence layer that turns a withdrawal request into completed stop/purge actions across every system and downstream party that holds the affected data.

P0Omnichannel Withdrawal IntakePatient (data principal)

Capture a withdrawal request through any channel the patient already uses — self-service portal/app, front-desk assisted, or phone — into a single tracked request.

As a patient, I want to withdraw my consent through the portal, the front desk, or a phone call so that I can stop processing of my data using whichever channel is easiest for me.
  • A patient can initiate withdrawal from the self-service portal/app without staff involvement, and the same request can alternatively be logged by front-desk staff or a phone agent on the patient's behalf.
  • Every withdrawal request, regardless of channel, produces one unified request record with a unique reference number shown/read back to the patient.
  • The withdrawal entry point is reachable in no more steps than the original consent capture (verifiable parity test of clicks/steps), satisfying 'as easy to withdraw as to give'.
  • Assisted (front-desk/phone) withdrawals require patient identity verification (e.g., ABHA/OTP or in-person ID) before the request is accepted.
P0Partial vs Full Withdrawal SelectorPatient (data principal)

Let the patient withdraw consent for specific purposes (e.g., research, marketing) while retaining others (e.g., active treatment), in plain language.

As a patient, I want to withdraw consent for some purposes (like research or marketing) but keep consent for my ongoing treatment so that I stay in control without disrupting my care.
  • The patient sees each active consent purpose as a separately selectable item and can withdraw one, several, or all of them in a single request.
  • Purposes that are legally required to continue (e.g., statutory medical record retention) are clearly flagged as not withdrawable, with the reason stated.
  • Purpose names and descriptions are shown in the patient's chosen language (Eighth Schedule set) at the point of selection.
  • The resulting request explicitly records which purposes are withdrawn and which are retained.
P0Withdrawal Impact AnalysisCompliance team / DPO

Automatically map a withdrawal to every internal system, dataset, and downstream party (processors, labs, third parties) that holds the affected data.

As a DPO, I want a withdrawal request to automatically show me which systems and external parties hold the affected data so that I know exactly what must stop or be purged.
  • On submission, the system generates an impact list of internal systems/datasets and named downstream parties linked to the withdrawn purpose(s).
  • Each impacted item shows its responsible owner and the required action (stop processing vs purge vs notify downstream).
  • Retained-purpose data and legally-retained data are excluded from purge actions and shown separately so they are not wrongly deleted.
  • The DPO can review the impact list before tasks are dispatched.
P0Task Routing with SLA DeadlinesHospital admin

Route each stop/purge/notify action to the responsible system owner as a tracked task with a due date and escalation if missed.

As a hospital admin, I want each withdrawal action routed to the right system owner with a deadline so that processing actually stops within the legally required time, not just on paper.
  • Each impacted item generates a task assigned to its owner with a configurable SLA due date derived from the withdrawal date.
  • Tasks approaching or past their deadline trigger reminders and escalate to a supervisor/DPO.
  • A live dashboard shows, per withdrawal, how many tasks are open, in-progress, completed, and overdue.
  • Downstream-party notification tasks track acknowledgement from the processor/third party, not just dispatch.
P0Stop/Purge Completion ReceiptsCompliance team / DPO

Capture proof of completion for each action and issue the patient a court-ready receipt confirming what was stopped or purged and when.

As a DPO, I want a verifiable receipt for every stop/purge action so that I can prove to the patient and the Board that the withdrawal was executed within time.
  • Each completed task records who completed it, when, and the action taken (stopped/purged/notified), with the completing owner's attestation.
  • When all in-scope tasks for a withdrawal are complete, the patient receives a confirmation receipt summarizing what was withdrawn and the actions taken.
  • A consolidated, tamper-evident withdrawal evidence package can be exported for any request showing the full timeline from intake to completion.
  • Downstream-party acknowledgements are included in the completion record.
Fast-follow & deferred
P1Patient Withdrawal Status Tracker— Give the patient real-time visibility into their withdrawal request status and SLA countdown via the portal and proactive notifications.Patient (data principal)
P1Guardian & Assisted Withdrawal for Minors and Dependents— Allow a verified guardian or authorized representative to withdraw consent on behalf of a minor or dependent, with proof of authority captured.Front-desk staff (capture consent)

Module 4 — Audit Evidence Vault5 P08 features

WHY THIS MODULE  This module exists to satisfy the Data Fiduciary's DPDP Act 2023 accountability and demonstrable-compliance obligation (Sections 5, 8(1)/8(2), 8(7)) — when a patient complains or the Data Protection Board investigates, the hospital must be able to PROVE what permission existed, when, for what purpose, and that it was honored, with tamper-evident evidence that holds up in front of the Board and courts.

P0Tamper-Evident Permission LedgerCompliance team / DPO

Append-only, hash-chained ledger that records every permission event (grant, scope, withdrawal, expiry, access) as an immutable entry.

As a DPO, I want every permission event written to an append-only hash-chained ledger so that no record can be silently altered or deleted and I can defend the hospital's compliance posture.
  • Every permission lifecycle event (grant, modify, withdraw, expire, purpose-use) creates a new ledger entry; existing entries can never be edited or deleted, only superseded.
  • Each entry is cryptographically chained to the prior entry so any tampering breaks the chain and is detectable.
  • Entries are timestamped with a trusted time source and attributed to the actor (patient, staff member, or system) who caused the event.
  • An integrity-check function can confirm the entire chain is intact and flag the exact entry where any break occurs.
P0Regulator & Court-Ready Evidence Package ExportCompliance team / DPO

One-click generation of a self-contained, signed evidence bundle for a patient or incident, ready to hand to the Data Protection Board or a court.

As a DPO responding to a Board inquiry, I want to export a complete signed evidence package for a patient in one click so that I can prove our permission handling without engineering help or manual assembly.
  • A user can generate a package scoped to one patient, one consent reference, or one incident/date-range and receive a single downloadable bundle.
  • The package includes the signed permission artifacts, the relevant ledger timeline, integrity proof, and a plain-language summary cover sheet.
  • The exported package is digitally signed/sealed and includes verification instructions a third party can follow to confirm authenticity offline.
  • The act of exporting is itself recorded in the access log with requester, scope, and reason.
P0Per-Patient Permission TimelineCompliance team / DPO

A single chronological view of everything that ever happened to a patient's permissions, retrievable by patient ID, ABHA, or consent reference.

As a DPO, I want a complete chronological timeline of a patient's permissions so that I can answer 'what did we have permission to do, and when' in seconds during a grievance or audit.
  • A user can pull the full timeline by patient identifier, linked ABHA number, or consent reference and see all grants, scopes, purposes, withdrawals, and accesses in order.
  • Each timeline entry shows what changed, who triggered it, the purpose it was tied to, and the language the patient saw at capture.
  • Withdrawal events and the resulting state change are clearly visible alongside the grant they revoke.
  • The timeline view can be exported directly into an evidence package.
P0Fast Evidence Retrieval & SearchCompliance team / DPO

High-speed search and filter across the vault by patient, ABHA, consent reference, purpose, date range, or event type.

As a DPO under a time-bound Board notice, I want to locate the exact permission evidence I need by any identifier or filter so that I can respond within regulatory deadlines.
  • A user can search by patient identifier, ABHA, consent reference, purpose, actor, event type, or date range and combine filters.
  • Search returns results fast enough to use interactively during an active inquiry, even on a large historical vault.
  • Search results link directly to the underlying ledger entries and the patient timeline.
  • Searches that surface patient records are themselves logged in the vault access log.
P0Vault Access Logging (Watch-the-Watchers)Compliance team / DPO

Immutable logging of every read, search, and export performed against the vault itself, so the evidence store is as accountable as the data it protects.

As a DPO, I want every access to the evidence vault recorded immutably so that I can prove the audit trail was not misused and so the Board trusts the evidence as untampered.
  • Every view, search, and export against the vault records who accessed it, when, what scope, and the stated reason.
  • Access logs are written to the same append-only, tamper-evident store and cannot be edited by vault users including admins.
  • A DPO can review and export an access report for any time window or for a specific patient's records.
Fast-follow & deferred
P1Integrity Proof & Verification— On-demand and scheduled integrity verification of the ledger, producing a portable proof that a given entry or package is genuine and unaltered.Compliance team / DPO
P1Retention & Lawful Disposal Alignment— Enforces retention rules so evidence is kept for the required period and disposed of in an auditable way, with the disposal itself logged as evidence.Compliance team / DPO
P2Multi-Language Evidence Rendering— Renders the evidence package and patient timeline in the Eighth Schedule language the patient was served, plus an English working copy for the Board.Compliance team / DPO

07 · OPD consent UX

Consent for a patient who barely uses a phone.

The hardest screen in the product isn't the compliance console — it's a consent flow for an anxious, possibly elderly patient in a crowded OPD waiting room, in a language that may not be English, on a device they don't own. v1 design principles:

Design principles

  • Language first — pick language before anything else.
  • Assume low literacy — icons, plain words, short lines, read-aloud audio.
  • Big touch targets — usable by shaky or unfamiliar hands.
  • Assisted mode — staff can drive on a shared device; still attributed to the patient.
  • Never default-on — every purpose is an explicit choice.
  • ~90 seconds — fast enough for OPD throughput.

Real-world constraints

  • Shared kiosk/tablet, not the patient's phone.
  • Noisy, time-pressured front desk.
  • Patient may be unwell, elderly, or accompanied by kin.
  • Many languages; uneven connectivity.
  • Must be provable later as that patient's consent.

The clickable version of this flow is in the case study.

08 · Tech-stack fit

How it sits in a hospital's stack — and on Certinal's.

The Hub is an execution layer that rides existing rails, not another silo staff must log into. Where it can, it surfaces inside the systems people already use (HIS screens, the patient portal) via embed/API. It is built on top of Certinal's e-signature, workflow and evidence engine — that's the unfair advantage, not a rebuild.

Integration sequence — lowest-risk value first

Capture + Audit

Need almost no integration — ship value (paperless consent, instant retrieval) before touching clinical systems.

HIS / registration embed

Trigger and surface consent capture inside the existing front-desk screen — no new login.

Withdrawal task routing

Wire the systems that hold data so withdrawals become real, tracked tasks.

EMR / LIS point-of-use enforcement

The deepest integration — check permission at the moment of access. Done last, on proven value.

ABDM cross-provider

Ride the national exchange rails for record sharing between providers.

Per scoping decision, this PRD stays at product/integration level — data models and API contracts are out of scope here and belong in the technical design that follows.

09 · v1 scope vs deferred

What ships, what waits, and why.

In v1

  • The four-step flow — the P0 features of all four modules.
  • Hospital-first — OPD/IPD consent, authorization, withdrawal, audit.
  • Vernacular, assisted capture — the OPD reality.
  • Certinal eSign + ABHA/OTP identity binding.
  • HIS / EMR / LIS integration for capture, enforcement and audit.

Deferred (with reason)

  • DSAR / rights console — real, but governance breadth; not the wedge.
  • Data discovery & inventory, DPIA, vendor risk — OneTrust/Securiti turf.
  • Cookie / web consent — one future channel of a unified preference layer.
  • Deep pharma/CRO clinical-trial eConsent — start hospital-first, expand later.
  • Retention/deletion automation — beyond withdrawal-driven stops.
10 · Metrics

How I'll know it's working.

Success metrics

  • Digital consent adoption — ~0% digital today → 90%+ in pilot depts by month 6 · Hub captures ÷ total registrations
  • Consent retrieval time — days today → <30s · timed from search to sealed export
  • Audit-prep effort — weeks today → −80% hours · person-hours per audit, before vs after
  • Workflow completion — n/a today → 95%+ closed within SLA · completed ÷ initiated authorizations
  • Monthly active compliance users — growing · weekly-active DPO/compliance seats

Failure signals

  • Staff still keep Excel trackers.
  • Teams still hold paper "just in case".
  • Audit retrieval still takes > 5 minutes.
  • Requests stall — low workflow completion.
11 · Rollout & pilot

Land narrow, prove value, then widen.

Design partner (0–3 mo)

One hospital chain, one site. Ship Consent Capture + Audit Vault — the lowest-integration, highest-proof slice.Exit: paper consent gone in one OPD; audit retrieval < 30s

Pilot (3–6 mo)

2–3 departments. Add Authorization for insurance/TPA and external data-share.Exit: 90% digital adoption in pilot depts

Withdrawal & enforcement (6–9 mo)

Turn on Withdrawal Management + point-of-use enforcement with EMR/LIS.Exit: withdrawals execute across systems with receipts

Chain-wide + diagnostics (9–15 mo)

Roll across the chain; extend to diagnostics. Begin templating for pharma/CRO.Exit: multi-site reference customer

12 · Risks, assumptions & open questions

What could break this — and what I'm assuming.

Risks → mitigation

  • Hospital integration is slow → start with capture + audit, which need the least integration.
  • "Another login" rejection → embed in HIS/portal, don't force a new tool.
  • Consent capture is commoditized → lead with execution + evidence, not signing.
  • Regulatory timing slips → the audit/retrieval value lands even pre-enforcement.

Assumptions & open questions

  • DPDP enforceable within ~12–16 months; rules clarify SDF duties.
  • Large chains likely classed as Significant Data Fiduciaries.
  • Open: Register as a DPDP Consent Manager later, or stay a processor for the Fiduciary?
  • Open: when do pharma/CRO clinical-trial flows earn their own track?
13 · Strategy (lighter)

Right to win, expansion, and go-to-market.

Right to win

Certinal already runs the hard primitives — e-sign, workflow, approvals, audit, evidence, integrations — plus healthcare e-consent and enterprise security (ISO 27001, SOC 2, FIPS). vs OneTrust/Securiti: generic and heavy, not clinical. vs in-house: hospitals don't want to build and maintain compliance plumbing.

Beyond India?

The four-step flow generalizes to other consent-heavy regulated verticals (BFSI, insurance) and to geographies with similar consent regimes (Gulf, SEA). But v1 is deliberately India-healthcare — win depth first, generalize later.

Pricing & adoption

Per-facility / volume SaaS + implementation, in a cost-sensitive, relationship-driven market. Land via compliance urgency and the CIO; distribute through HIS vendors and SIs; drive adoption by embedding in the existing front-desk workflow (low training) and proving audit-prep savings.
